# 🗂️ Role: Receptionist (Front Desk)
> **Scope:** Patient registration, check-in, queue management, appointment scheduling, and front-desk coordination.  
> **Accountability:** Operational integrity. You are the first point of contact — errors here cascade through every downstream role.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Patient Registration
- **DO** search for an existing patient record before creating a new one — duplicate records corrupt clinical history and billing.
- **DO** capture all required registration fields: full legal name, DOB, national ID/passport, gender, phone, next of kin, and insurance details.
- **DO** verify the patient's identity document physically before creating or modifying their record.
- **DO** confirm insurance or SHA scheme eligibility at registration — flag unverified insurance to the cashier immediately.
- **DO** assign the correct visit type (outpatient, inpatient, emergency) at check-in — this affects queue routing, billing, and clinical workflows.

### Queue Management
- **DO** issue queue tickets immediately after check-in is complete and the patient is confirmed in the system.
- **DO** route patients to the correct department based on their presenting complaint and triage priority.
- **DO** communicate wait time estimates honestly and update patients if there are delays.
- **DO** flag emergency presentations to nursing triage immediately — do not queue them normally.
- **DO** escalate queue congestion issues to the duty nurse or supervisor rather than making unilateral routing decisions.

### Appointments & Scheduling
- **DO** confirm appointment details with the patient verbally and via SMS/system notification at booking.
- **DO** check doctor availability and branch schedule before booking a slot.
- **DO** record cancellations and rescheduling in the system immediately to free up slots.
- **DO** send appointment reminders where the system supports it — reduce no-shows proactively.

### Data Accuracy
- **DO** correct data entry errors through the proper amendment workflow — not by overwriting raw fields silently.
- **DO** update contact details and next-of-kin info whenever a patient volunteers a change.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Registration Errors
- **DON'T** create a new patient record if one already exists — search first with at least two identifiers (name + DOB, or ID number).
- **DON'T** guess or estimate patient details — if a patient can't provide information, document "not provided" and flag for follow-up.
- **DON'T** register a patient under a wrong visit type to "save time" — fixing downstream billing and clinical errors takes far longer.
- **DON'T** process walk-in registrations during scheduled appointment slots in a way that unfairly displaces booked patients without supervisor authorization.

### Queue & Workflow
- **DON'T** manually re-order or manipulate the queue except under explicit supervisor instruction — queue integrity affects clinical prioritization.
- **DON'T** send a patient to a department before their registration and check-in are fully complete in the system.
- **DON'T** dismiss or minimize a patient who reports worsening symptoms while waiting — escalate to nursing immediately.
- **DON'T** promise specific consultation times — you manage scheduling, not clinical throughput.

### Data & Privacy
- **DON'T** share patient contact details, visit history, or any personal information with walk-in inquirers without verifying their relationship and getting consent.
- **DON'T** access patient clinical records (diagnoses, prescriptions, lab results) — your view is limited to registration and scheduling data.
- **DON'T** leave the reception terminal unattended and logged in — lock the screen every time you step away.
- **DON'T** share your credentials with a colleague to "cover" the desk — each person must log in as themselves.

### Financial
- **DON'T** accept or handle payments — all financial transactions go through the cashier.
- **DON'T** make insurance eligibility determinations — flag to cashier and let the system verify.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **A duplicate patient record is a clinical hazard** — a doctor prescribing without access to a patient's full history can cause harm.
- Your job ends at the front desk — resist the urge to make clinical or financial decisions that belong to other roles.
- The queue is a clinical tool, not just an admin list. Treat it with the same seriousness as a clinical record.
