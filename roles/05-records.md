# 📁 Role: Records (Medical Records Officer)
> **Scope:** Management, retrieval, archiving, and integrity of all patient medical records — past and present.  
> **Accountability:** Legal and compliance. Medical records are legal documents. Errors, omissions, or unauthorized disclosures are actionable offenses.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Record Management
- **DO** maintain a single, unified patient record per person — deduplicate aggressively using at least two identifiers before merging any records.
- **DO** ensure all clinical documentation is filed against the correct patient and the correct visit — misfiled records are as dangerous as missing records.
- **DO** archive completed patient records per the hospital's retention policy (Kenya standard: minimum 7 years for adults, until age 21 for minors).
- **DO** flag incomplete records (missing discharge summaries, unsigned orders, missing lab results) and route them back to the responsible clinician for completion within the defined SLA.
- **DO** maintain a register of all records released — internal and external — with date, recipient, and purpose.

### Record Requests & Releases
- **DO** verify the identity and authorization of anyone requesting patient records before releasing anything — patient, legal guardian, authorized third party, or court order.
- **DO** process records requests within the facility's defined turnaround time (typically 48–72 hours for non-urgent requests).
- **DO** use the system's official records release workflow — no informal handoffs, no email attachments of PHI without encryption.
- **DO** document every records release in the system with: requester identity, relationship to patient, purpose, date, and what was released.
- **DO** redact sensitive sections (e.g., HIV status, mental health notes) where legally required before releasing to third parties.

### Data Integrity
- **DO** conduct periodic audits of record completeness — especially for inpatient discharge summaries and surgical records.
- **DO** report any suspected tampering, unauthorized access, or record destruction to the superadmin and hospital management immediately.
- **DO** ensure digital records have a clear version history — any amendment must be logged with who changed it, when, and why. Never overwrite.

### Compliance
- **DO** stay current with Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019) and any relevant health records regulations.
- **DO** escalate any court orders or legal holds on patient records to hospital management and legal counsel immediately — do not act unilaterally.
- **DO** maintain confidentiality of all records you handle, including records you access incidentally while performing your duties.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Record Integrity
- **DON'T** alter, edit, or delete any clinical documentation — your role is custodian, not author. Corrections must go through the originating clinician via an addendum workflow.
- **DON'T** merge patient records without dual verification — a wrongly merged record can cause a patient to receive another person's treatment history.
- **DON'T** permanently delete any patient record regardless of age or apparent inactivity — archive, never destroy, unless under explicit legal mandate with documented authorization.
- **DON'T** leave incomplete records unresolved — chase them systematically. An incomplete record is a legal liability.

### Access & Disclosure
- **DON'T** release patient records to family members, employers, insurance companies, or police without proper authorization — verbal requests don't count.
- **DON'T** share records via personal email, WhatsApp, or any non-secured channel.
- **DON'T** access patient records out of curiosity — only access records you have an active operational reason to view.
- **DON'T** confirm or deny a patient's presence at the facility to unauthorized inquirers.

### System Boundaries
- **DON'T** create or modify clinical entries (diagnoses, prescriptions, orders) — your access is read and retrieve, not write on clinical data.
- **DON'T** process financial records or billing adjustments — that is the cashier's domain.
- **DON'T** share your system credentials — records access logs are tied to your account and you bear legal responsibility for what happens under your login.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **Medical records are legal instruments** — every entry you touch or release is a potential exhibit in court.
- "I was asked to" is not a defense for an unauthorized records release. Verify authorization, document it, then release.
- Your job is to protect record integrity first, and facilitate access second — in that order, always.
