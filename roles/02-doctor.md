# 🩺 Role: Doctor (Clinician)
> **Scope:** Clinical decision-making, diagnosis, prescriptions, lab/radiology orders, and patient discharge.  
> **Accountability:** Clinical and legal. Every order placed, diagnosis written, and prescription issued carries your medical license.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Patient Management
- **DO** review the full patient history — previous visits, allergies, chronic conditions — before opening a new consultation.
- **DO** document every consultation with structured, complete notes: chief complaint, history, examination findings, assessment, and plan (SOAP format or equivalent).
- **DO** confirm patient identity before starting any consultation — name, DOB, and patient ID.
- **DO** update the patient's problem list and allergy records every visit if new information surfaces.
- **DO** mark a consultation as complete only after all orders (lab, radiology, pharmacy) have been placed and notes are finalized.

### Prescriptions & Orders
- **DO** prescribe using the system's approved drug formulary — it has dose range checks and allergy alerts built in.
- **DO** specify dose, frequency, route, and duration explicitly on every prescription — no ambiguous shorthand.
- **DO** check for drug-drug and drug-allergy interactions using the system's alerts before confirming a prescription.
- **DO** place lab and radiology orders with a clear clinical indication — vague orders waste resources and delay results.
- **DO** review lab and radiology results within the system and attach your interpretation to the patient's file before discharge.

### Queue & Workflow
- **DO** only start a consultation ticket when you are physically ready to see the patient — don't hold tickets idle.
- **DO** follow the pipeline forward-only rule: you cannot re-open a department stage that has already been completed in the current visit.
- **DO** communicate clearly in the system if a patient needs urgent re-routing (e.g., emergency referral to theatre).

### Communication & Referrals
- **DO** use the in-system referral module for internal department transfers — don't verbally redirect patients without logging it.
- **DO** document the reason for external referrals and what the receiving facility needs to know.
- **DO** update discharge status and instructions in the system before the patient leaves — cashier and pharmacy depend on this.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Clinical Safety
- **DON'T** prescribe medications outside the system — verbal or paper prescriptions that bypass the HMS break the audit trail and create dangerous gaps.
- **DON'T** ignore system-generated drug interaction or allergy warnings without documenting your clinical override reason.
- **DON'T** finalize a diagnosis or discharge a patient without reviewing pending lab or radiology results — unless you explicitly document why you're proceeding.
- **DON'T** write ambiguous prescription instructions (e.g., "as directed") — be explicit every single time.

### Data & Privacy
- **DON'T** access patient records outside your assigned branch or queue unless you have an explicit cross-branch authorization.
- **DON'T** share login credentials — every action on a patient's record is tied to your account and your medical license.
- **DON'T** take screenshots or external copies of patient data — use the system's report and referral export features.
- **DON'T** discuss patient details in public areas or unsecured channels.

### Workflow Violations
- **DON'T** close a consultation ticket without completing all required fields — incomplete records block downstream roles (pharmacy, lab, cashier).
- **DON'T** attempt to modify billing or financial records — that is strictly the cashier's domain.
- **DON'T** bypass the queue system by pulling patients in outside of the assigned order without supervisor approval.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **You own the clinical outcome** — the system helps you but cannot replace your judgment.
- Incomplete documentation is the #1 source of medical-legal risk. If it's not in the system, it didn't happen.
- The queue pipeline is forward-only by design — respect it, don't work around it.
