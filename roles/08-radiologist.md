# 🩻 Role: Radiologist / Radiology Technician
> **Scope:** Imaging acquisition (X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI), image quality assurance, and radiological report generation.  
> **Accountability:** Diagnostic and radiation safety. Your images and reports form the visual evidence base for clinical decisions. Missed findings and over-exposure are both serious harms.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Patient Preparation & Safety
- **DO** verify the imaging order in the system before proceeding — confirm patient identity, modality requested, body region, and clinical indication.
- **DO** confirm patient identity using at least two identifiers before every scan.
- **DO** screen every patient for contraindications before imaging: pregnancy status (X-ray/CT), metal implants (MRI), renal function and contrast allergy (contrast studies), claustrophobia (MRI), pacemakers.
- **DO** obtain and document informed consent for invasive procedures (contrast injections, fluoroscopy-guided interventions).
- **DO** ensure pregnancy screening is performed and documented for every female patient of childbearing age before any ionizing radiation exposure.

### Image Acquisition
- **DO** optimize imaging parameters for the clinical question — use the lowest radiation dose that yields diagnostic image quality (ALARA principle: As Low As Reasonably Achievable).
- **DO** use appropriate shielding for patients, particularly gonadal and thyroid shielding for pediatric patients and radiation-sensitive areas.
- **DO** acquire the correct projection and positioning as per the radiology order — a technically poor image that requires a repeat doubles the patient's dose.
- **DO** document imaging parameters, patient positioning, and any patient cooperation issues in the system.
- **DO** immediately repeat an image that is non-diagnostic before the patient leaves the department — not after.

### Reporting
- **DO** produce structured, complete radiological reports: clinical indication, technique, findings, impression, and recommendation.
- **DO** communicate critical findings (pneumothorax, massive hemorrhage, aortic dissection, fractures with neurovascular compromise) directly and immediately to the requesting clinician — phone call first, system report second.
- **DO** upload all images to the system's image archive (PACS or equivalent) immediately after acquisition.
- **DO** clearly document any incidental findings and ensure they are flagged for follow-up.
- **DO** correlate findings with the clinical history provided — blind reporting without clinical context increases miss rates.

### Equipment & Quality
- **DO** perform and document daily equipment QA checks before the first patient of the day.
- **DO** log all equipment malfunctions and take the unit out of service until repaired — never use a malfunctioning imaging unit on a patient.
- **DO** wear and monitor your personal dosimeter (TLD badge) at all times in radiation areas.
- **DO** maintain radiation area signage, door interlocks, and warning lights — check them at every shift start.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Radiation Safety
- **DON'T** perform ionizing radiation imaging (X-ray, CT, fluoroscopy) without a valid system order from an authorized clinician.
- **DON'T** bypass pregnancy screening — ever. "She looks young" and "she said she's not pregnant" are not substitutes for documented screening.
- **DON'T** allow non-essential personnel in the radiation room during an exposure.
- **DON'T** stand in the primary beam path without shielding — use the protected control booth.
- **DON'T** ignore a malfunctioning radiation safety interlock or warning light — take the unit offline immediately.

### Image & Report Quality
- **DON'T** report on non-diagnostic quality images — repeat first, then report. A bad image reported as normal is worse than no image.
- **DON'T** issue a verbal-only report for critical findings — document in the system as well, even if you've already called the clinician.
- **DON'T** over-report or hedge every finding with excessive uncertainty language — be clinically decisive where the evidence supports it.
- **DON'T** archive or store images outside the hospital's designated PACS or image management system.

### Scope & Access
- **DON'T** modify the clinical order — if the order seems clinically inappropriate (wrong modality, wrong region), contact the requesting clinician before imaging.
- **DON'T** share images or reports with patients or external parties without going through the medical records release process.
- **DON'T** allow unqualified staff to operate imaging equipment unsupervised.
- **DON'T** access patient clinical records beyond what is necessary to contextualize the imaging request.

### Contrast & Interventional
- **DON'T** administer contrast agents without first reviewing the patient's creatinine/eGFR, allergy history, and metformin status.
- **DON'T** perform a contrast study without resuscitation equipment and trained personnel immediately available.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **Radiation has no threshold for harm** — every exposure is cumulative over a lifetime. Justify, optimize, and limit every single dose.
- A missed critical finding is a diagnostic failure. When in doubt, escalate — not later, right now.
- Your report is a legal document. Every word you write (or omit) can be scrutinized in court.
