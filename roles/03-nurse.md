# 💉 Role: Nurse
> **Scope:** Triage, vitals capture, nursing assessment, medication administration (as prescribed), patient monitoring, and care execution.  
> **Accountability:** Clinical execution. You are the bridge between the doctor's orders and the patient's body.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Triage & Assessment
- **DO** capture and record vitals (BP, temp, pulse, SpO2, weight, RBS) accurately and completely at every triage encounter — these values drive clinical decisions.
- **DO** flag critical vitals immediately via the system's alert mechanism and verbally to the attending clinician — don't just log and wait.
- **DO** document the triage category (emergency, urgent, non-urgent) and time-stamp the assessment.
- **DO** re-assess vitals after any intervention or if a patient's condition changes while in the waiting area.
- **DO** use the nursing assessment module to document pain scores, presenting complaint, and patient-reported history.

### Medication Administration
- **DO** follow the 5 Rights every time before administering any medication: Right Patient, Right Drug, Right Dose, Right Route, Right Time.
- **DO** verify the prescription in the system before administering — never administer from a verbal order alone without a corresponding system entry.
- **DO** record medication administration in the system immediately after giving it — not later, not "when you get time."
- **DO** document any patient refusals or missed doses with the reason.
- **DO** check the patient's allergy flag in the system before administering any drug.

### Patient Care & Monitoring
- **DO** update inpatient observation notes at the defined frequency (per ward protocol) — continuously for ICU, at defined intervals for general wards.
- **DO** document wound care, IV site changes, catheter care, and other nursing procedures with time and outcome.
- **DO** escalate any deterioration in patient condition using the system's escalation flag — and simultaneously notify the doctor directly.
- **DO** complete nursing handover notes before ending your shift — the incoming nurse depends on your documentation.

### Queue & Workflow
- **DO** confirm patient identity before every procedure — name and patient ID minimum.
- **DO** update the patient's queue status when you begin and complete nursing interventions.
- **DO** communicate actively with the receptionist if the queue is backing up due to a clinical reason.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Clinical Boundaries
- **DON'T** diagnose — you may observe and report, but diagnosis is the doctor's domain. Document what you see, not what you conclude.
- **DON'T** alter or countermand a doctor's orders unilaterally — if you believe an order is unsafe, escalate through proper channels and document your concern.
- **DON'T** administer a medication not found in the system prescription list for that patient — no exceptions.
- **DON'T** skip the allergy check even for "routine" medications — familiarity breeds complacency, and complacency kills.

### Documentation
- **DON'T** document vitals or interventions you didn't personally observe or perform — never falsify records.
- **DON'T** leave nursing fields blank because "nothing changed" — explicitly document "no change noted" with a timestamp.
- **DON'T** use informal language or abbreviations in clinical notes that could be misread by another clinician.

### Data & Privacy
- **DON'T** access patient records beyond your assigned ward or triage station without authorization.
- **DON'T** share patient information with family members without explicit patient consent documented in the system.
- **DON'T** share your login credentials under any circumstances — every drug administration and nursing note is legally tied to your account.

### Workflow
- **DON'T** discharge a patient from nursing care before the doctor has completed and signed the discharge order in the system.
- **DON'T** modify pharmacy or billing entries — route all corrections through the appropriate role.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **You are the patient's last safety check** before any medication reaches them — act like it.
- Shift handover documentation is not optional — a nurse who doesn't hand over properly is a patient safety risk.
- "I was busy" is never a valid reason for late or missing documentation in a clinical setting.
