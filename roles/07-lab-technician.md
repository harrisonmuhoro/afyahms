# 🔬 Role: Laboratory Technician (Lab)
> **Scope:** Sample collection, processing, analysis, result reporting, and lab quality control.  
> **Accountability:** Diagnostic accuracy. Clinicians make life-and-death decisions based on your results. An error here ripples through the entire clinical chain.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Sample Collection & Handling
- **DO** verify the lab order in the system before collecting any sample — confirm patient identity, test requested, and requesting clinician.
- **DO** confirm patient identity using at least two identifiers (name + patient ID or DOB) before every sample collection.
- **DO** use the correct sample collection tube/container for each test — wrong anticoagulant or container invalidates results.
- **DO** label samples at the point of collection, not at the bench — every unlabeled or post-hoc-labeled sample is a patient safety risk.
- **DO** document collection time accurately — time-sensitive tests (cortisol, glucose, cultures) depend on it.
- **DO** follow correct order of draw for multi-tube collections to prevent cross-contamination.

### Processing & Analysis
- **DO** process samples within the required stability window — log any delays and their clinical impact.
- **DO** run quality control (QC) checks before running patient samples on any analyzer — never skip QC to "save time."
- **DO** flag any QC failures immediately and take corrective action before reporting any results from that run.
- **DO** repeat any test with results that are implausible or inconsistent with the patient's clinical picture — then report both results with a note.
- **DO** follow standard operating procedures (SOPs) for every test — no improvisation on methodology.

### Result Reporting
- **DO** enter results into the system promptly — clinicians and patients are waiting on these for treatment decisions.
- **DO** flag critical/panic values immediately by system alert AND by direct verbal notification to the requesting clinician — do not rely on the system alert alone.
- **DO** include reference ranges on all reported results.
- **DO** clearly mark any result that has been amended, and document the reason for the amendment.
- **DO** retain raw data (analyzer printouts, gel images, culture plates) per the lab's retention policy.

### Quality & Safety
- **DO** wear appropriate PPE for every sample type — treat every sample as potentially infectious.
- **DO** decontaminate work surfaces before and after every session and after any spill.
- **DO** dispose of sharps, biohazardous materials, and chemical waste per the facility's waste management protocol.
- **DO** participate in External Quality Assessment (EQA) programs and document proficiency testing results.
- **DO** calibrate and maintain all equipment per manufacturer schedules and log every maintenance event.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Sample Integrity
- **DON'T** process or report results from an unlabeled, mislabeled, or improperly collected sample — reject and re-collect, document why.
- **DON'T** swap or share sample labels — ever. This is the single fastest way to cause a catastrophic patient harm event.
- **DON'T** delay reporting critical values to "confirm with another run first" without notifying the clinician of the pending confirmation.
- **DON'T** guess patient details or assume identity — always verify before collection.

### Quality Control
- **DON'T** report patient results if QC has failed — quarantine the run, fix the issue, re-run QC, then report.
- **DON'T** backdate or falsify QC records — if QC was missed or failed, document it honestly.
- **DON'T** use reagents or kits past their expiry date, even if stock is low.
- **DON'T** use an analyzer that is overdue for calibration or maintenance — flag it as out of service.

### Scope & Access
- **DON'T** interpret results clinically or suggest diagnoses to patients — report findings, let the clinician interpret.
- **DON'T** release results directly to patients or family — all results go through the requesting clinician via the system.
- **DON'T** modify a doctor's lab order — if clarification is needed, contact the clinician.
- **DON'T** access patient clinical records beyond the lab order and relevant clinical context needed to process the test.

### Safety
- **DON'T** recap needles — use the one-hand technique or a needle-capping device if recapping is unavoidable.
- **DON'T** eat, drink, or apply cosmetics in the lab working area.
- **DON'T** ignore a needle-stick or exposure incident — report it immediately per the facility's post-exposure protocol.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **A wrong result is worse than no result** — it gives a clinician false confidence to make a bad decision.
- Critical values are not a routine notification. They are an emergency communication. Treat them as such.
- QC is not bureaucracy — it is the only thing standing between your analyzer's output and a patient's treatment plan.
