# 💊 Role: Pharmacist
> **Scope:** Prescription verification, drug dispensing, inventory management, patient medication counseling, and drug safety oversight.  
> **Accountability:** Clinical and legal. A dispensing error can kill. Every drug that leaves this department is your professional responsibility.

---

## ✅ DO's

### Prescription Verification
- **DO** verify every prescription in the system before dispensing — confirm it was issued by an authorized, licensed clinician for this patient on this visit.
- **DO** cross-check the prescribed drug against the patient's documented allergy list before dispensing.
- **DO** check for drug-drug interactions across the patient's entire current medication list — not just the new prescription in isolation.
- **DO** verify dose appropriateness against the patient's weight, age, renal function, and liver status where relevant.
- **DO** query or escalate any prescription that appears clinically inappropriate — you have both the right and the duty to do so before dispensing.

### Dispensing
- **DO** dispense the exact drug, formulation, strength, and quantity specified in the prescription — no substitutions without documented prescriber approval.
- **DO** label every dispensed medication with: patient name, drug name, dose, frequency, route, duration, and dispensing date.
- **DO** counsel every patient on how to take their medication, what to expect, and what side effects warrant an immediate return.
- **DO** record dispensing in the system immediately — quantity out, batch number, and expiry date.
- **DO** confirm patient identity before handing over any medication.

### Inventory Management
- **DO** conduct stock counts at defined intervals (daily for controlled substances, weekly for high-value drugs, monthly for general stock).
- **DO** flag low-stock levels in the system before they become stockouts — proactive reorder, not reactive scramble.
- **DO** quarantine and report expired, damaged, or suspicious drugs immediately — never dispense them to "use them up."
- **DO** follow the FEFO (First Expiry, First Out) principle strictly for all dispensing.
- **DO** maintain a register for all controlled/narcotic substances with dual-signature verification for every transaction.

### Compliance & Safety
- **DO** report adverse drug reactions (ADRs) observed in patients through the system and to the pharmacy regulatory body.
- **DO** store drugs under the correct conditions — temperature-sensitive drugs must be in designated cold storage, never on the counter.
- **DO** participate in drug formulary reviews and flag availability issues that could affect patient care.

---

## ❌ DON'Ts

### Dispensing Errors
- **DON'T** dispense any drug without a valid system prescription — verbal requests from doctors, nurses, or patients are not acceptable without a corresponding system entry.
- **DON'T** substitute a prescribed drug with a different drug, generic, or formulation without explicit prescriber approval documented in the system.
- **DON'T** dispense a drug you have a clinical concern about without first raising the concern with the prescribing doctor — then document the outcome either way.
- **DON'T** dispense without counseling — handing a bag of drugs to a patient without instructions is not dispensing, it's distribution.
- **DON'T** ignore allergy flags or interaction alerts — document your reasoning if you proceed, but never silently override.

### Inventory & Stock
- **DON'T** dispense expired or damaged drugs under any circumstance — including "emergencies."
- **DON'T** allow stock adjustments without a proper system entry and justification — unexplained inventory variances are a red flag for theft or diversion.
- **DON'T** store controlled substances without dual-lock security and a maintained narcotics register.
- **DON'T** accept drug deliveries without verifying against the purchase order, checking expiry dates, and confirming cold-chain compliance for temperature-sensitive items.

### Scope & Access
- **DON'T** modify prescription orders — if an order needs to be changed, route the change back to the prescribing doctor.
- **DON'T** access patient records beyond what is necessary to verify the prescription and dispensing history.
- **DON'T** share dispensing credentials — every dispensing event is tied to your pharmacy license.
- **DON'T** allow non-pharmacy staff to access the dispensing counter or drug storage areas unsupervised.

---

## ⚠️ Critical Reminders
- **The right drug to the wrong patient, or the wrong drug to the right patient — both are equally dangerous.** Verify identity every single time.
- Inventory discrepancies don't resolve themselves. Investigate every one, no matter how small.
- Your professional license is on the line with every dispensing decision — act accordingly.
